# Pipeline and Selection, redesigned · issues 0083 and 0089 (asks from 0067 and 0071)

Juan found the shared table introduced for 0067/0071 harder to read than the pipeline it
replaced, and the selection page built on it no better. Both pages are redesigned by Claude:
the pipeline from its look before 27 Sep, and selection from scratch after the M03 board.
Tonight's data fixes stay as they were: every pursuit is listed, scores are specific to the
vehicle, and the bulk actions are audited and idempotent.

**Pipeline.** The status cards are back, mono label over a Fraunces count, and all eight fit
one row: the seven statuses and All, after Passed. They narrow with the page and scroll sideways
rather than wrap when too narrow to read. The search and six thin dropdowns, with labels above
them, share one line at laptop width. The table starts in score order and every column sorts;
tapping again reverses. The score shows as a number with a short bar and says where it comes
from: a fit assessment, or a provisional score, marked when it is stale. An organisation that is
the LP leads its row with its person under it. When several people from one organisation are
pursued, the organisation heads a group and its people sit beneath it on a thread, each with
their own status and evidence. Small icons from the rest of the app mark people, organisations,
routes, meetings and money. As the page narrows, capacity, routes, owner, their read, meetings
and last touch move under the LP's name instead of pushing the evidence ladder out of view. A row
opens the LP. Rows can be ticked, and the actions for the ticked LPs (status, touchpoint, context,
requests for research, connections or strategy, and feedback) appear in the right-hand pane,
or in the page when the pane is closed.

**Selection.** A ranked list sits beside the reasons for the LP in focus. Each row shows its
rank, the name (the organisation's name first where the organisation is the LP), a line of
what matters for choosing, and the score. Those details are the organisation or person, status,
capacity, routes, meetings, last touch, money, flags and the strategy's list (this year or 2027).
New and Sourcing are shown to start. Each status is a toggle with its count, and All turns every
status on, or back to the two. The list can be ordered by score, capacity, routes, last touch or
name. The reasons panel reads, when an LP comes into focus, the fit assessment's graded dimensions
or the strategy's four readings. Each reading shows its weight, a bar, and the sentence it rests
on. The panel also shows the angle, the best path, the ask, the next step, contact, evidence and
flags, with links to the LP, its fit and its routes. Unknown readings say so and are not counted.
Ticked LPs get the same actions, starting on "Set status → Selected". On a narrow page, the reasons
open under the LP in focus.

**Data and size.** Rows now carry the strategy's list and the first three flags, each cut to 160
characters, with a count of the rest. The full list is on the LP page and in the selection
reasons. That took the real-copy pages from about 3.1 to 2.6–2.9 MB, or about 310–330 KB
compressed. A new read-only `scoreDetail` in `lib/pipeline-data.ts` answers the reasons panel.
It returns nothing for a pursuit outside the vehicle asked about. The styles live in
`components/strategy/lp-tables.module.css`, and the 0067/0071 block has left `app/globals.css`.

Validation: typecheck and boundaries pass, and 442 of 442 properties hold, one of them new: the
reasons show each reading behind a provisional score, and only inside their vehicle. Checked in
Chromium at 1440×900 and 1800×1100, at iPad width 1180×820 with touch, and in a 456 px content
column. Both pages were checked on the demo and on a copy of the real data. Timed on the copy
through the page benchmark: pipeline 0.33 s first and 0.27 s repeat, selection 0.18 and 0.21 s,
the all-vehicles pipeline 2.07 and 0.31 s. A search keystroke over about 2,000 LPs draws in
40–260 ms on the development server. The app shell has no phone layout: at 390 px the rail keeps
its width on every page. That is outside these pages. Safari was not available, so iPad Safari
itself is unverified. No screenshots or real records are included.
