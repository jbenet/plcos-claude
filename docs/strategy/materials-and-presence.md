# Public presence and marketing: PLC Neurotech I and the Netholabs SPV, compared with similar funds

Research pass, 28 Sep 2026, ahead of LP outreach starting 29 Sep 2026.
Scope, as revised by Juan: the outreach materials (deck, one-pagers) already exist, so this
file does not cover them. It covers three things: (1) what the public can see about us
today, (2) how comparable funds present themselves, and (3) a 1–3 month plan for building
credibility and inbound interest, including what Rule 506(c) allows.

Method and limits:
- Only public web pages were used. Nothing was signed into, paid for, posted or submitted,
  and no LinkedIn pages were fetched.
- Pages were read through a summarising fetch tool. Counts and quotes are as the page showed
  them on 28 Sep 2026.
- Subscriber numbers are what each publisher claims about itself. None were independently
  checked.
- Items marked **(low confidence)** come from a search-result snippet or a page that could
  not be read in full.
- Every source is listed in the table at the end as [S#], with its URL and the date read
  (28 Sep 2026 for all of them).
- This is general information, not legal advice. Items marked **counsel** need review by
  fund counsel before anything is published.

Related internal research: `docs/05-marketing-content-reputation.md` covers channel
economics. Its §7 draws the publishing line for a 506(b) fund. Section 4 below updates that
line for 506(c).

### Patterns across the comparables

1. **An annual data report is the strongest lasting asset.**
   - Air Street credits its *State of AI* report in its own Fund III announcement [S37].
   - Joyance gets cited as a neurotech investor through an annual outlook [S28].
   - In neurotech that slot is currently held by media, not investors: Neurofounders (the
     H1 review and the investor map) and Neurotech Futures [S15], [S17], [S11].
2. **A public fund-announcement post is an LP document.**
   - Air Street and Not Boring both publish structured posts covering thesis, portfolio,
     terms and community [S37], [S45].
   - Under 506(c), Not Boring attached an interest form [S45].
3. **The funds with the most content run research as a staffed function with its own name.**
   Dimension has a research team and a research Substack [S31], [S32]. Air Street has Air
   Street Press [S38].
4. **Portfolio pages show outcomes and stories, not just logos.** JAZZ labels exits [S25];
   Kaleida publishes an exit case study [S27].
5. **There is a visible way in for investors.** JAZZ has an LP Portal [S25], Kaleida a "For
   Investors" page [S26], Lux an IR@ address [S40], and age1 an FAQ [S34].
6. **Events produce public outputs.** RAAIS has run since 2017 [S37]. Riskgaming materials
   are free to download [S41]. Fifty Years runs grants and a playbook [S43]. By contrast, PL
   Neuro's three events are invite-only and published nothing [S7].
7. **Each fund close gets press.** Dimension's three closes were covered by STAT and
   BioPharma Dive [S32], [S33]; Air Street's close by TechCrunch [S39]; age1's launch went
   out on Business Wire [S35].

## 4. What 506(c) allows for public marketing (and where counsel is needed)

**What the rule says.**
- **General solicitation is allowed.** The offering may be promoted publicly, provided (1)
  every purchaser is accredited and (2) the issuer takes "reasonable steps to verify"
  that. A Form D is due within 15 days of the first sale [S47].
- **Verification became easier in March 2025.** An SEC staff no-action letter (12 Mar 2025)
  accepts a **minimum investment of $200,000 for individuals or $1M for entities** as a
  reasonable verification step, provided three things hold:
  - the purchaser gives written representations that they are accredited and that the
    investment is not financed by a third party;
  - the issuer has no actual knowledge to the contrary;
  - the minimum is met. **Binding capital commitments count toward the minimum** [S48],
    [S49], [S50].

**What this opens up for us, subject to counsel:**
- Saying publicly that PLC Neurotech I and the Netholabs SPV are raising.
- An investor page on plcapital.xyz with an "express interest" form.
- A public fund-announcement post (the Not Boring template [S45]).
- Answering "are you raising?" on a podcast. That was the trap under 506(b) (doc 05 §7).
- Posting on X or LinkedIn about the raise.
- Mentioning the raise in a newsletter.

**Rules that still apply however we market:**
- **Anti-fraud.** Rule 206(4)-8 prohibits false or misleading statements to investors or
  prospective investors in pooled vehicles. It applies to unregistered advisers, including
  exempt reporting advisers [S51], [S52].
- **The SEC Marketing Rule** (206(4)-1) is stated to apply to advisers "registered or
  required to be registered" [S53]. As an exempt reporting adviser, PLC Neurotech appears
  to fall outside it [S54]. **Counsel** should confirm this, and we should still present
  performance in a way the rule would accept.

**Counsel review needed before publishing:**
1. **Form D and consistency.** Confirm that each vehicle's Form D is filed under 506(c) and
   that no vehicle has relied on 506(b) at any point. Anything published must match the
   PPM and LPA: size, terms and close dates.
2. **Track record.**
   - How to show the neurotech track record from before the fund: Protocol Labs or
     personal investments versus the fund's own.
   - Whether gross or net figures are required.
   - How to avoid selection bias in which portfolio companies are shown.
   - The page already calls the adviser "newly formed" [S2].
3. **Portfolio logos and stories.** Get company consent, and state whether each company is
   in the fund or a prior investment. The existing "not a complete list" legend [S3]
   helps, but it is not enough on its own for stories.
4. **Netholabs SPV.**
   - Public marketing of a single named private company needs the company's consent. Its
     confidential information stays in the verified-investor data room.
   - Netholabs is a UK company [S22]. Marketing to UK or EU persons falls under local
     financial-promotion rules, which 506(c) does not override.
5. **Non-US audiences in general.** 506(c) is a US exemption. Public posts reach everyone.
6. **Qualified purchaser status.** If any vehicle relies on 3(c)(7), qualified-purchaser
   status is a separate test from accreditation.
7. **State notice filings** for 506 offerings.
8. **Testimonials or endorsements** from founders or LPs, even though the Marketing Rule may
   not apply.
9. **A written publishing policy.** Replace doc 05's 506(b) two-tier policy with a 506(c)
   one. The question stops being "never say we're raising" and becomes: is it accurate, is
   it consistent with the offering documents, does it carry the standard legend, and does
   every purchaser go through verification? Keep the mechanical checklist.
